  <h2>Our Story</h2>
  <p>The name <em>La Loba</em> comes from a Mexican folktale made widely known through Clarissa Pinkola Estés' <em>Women Who Run with the Wolves</em>.</p>
  <p>In the story, La Loba — the Wolf Woman — wanders the desert gathering bones and singing them back to life. What resonates with us is not the bones themselves, but what they represent: the idea that even in the face of loss, there is still beauty, meaning, connection, and wholeness.</p>
  <p>Too often, our culture approaches aging, serious illness, caregiving, and death through the lens of fear and avoidance. Yet these experiences are also deeply human transitions — times when relationships, values, stories, and legacies come into sharper focus.</p>
  <p>At La Loba Pathways, we believe that understanding the path ahead can lessen fear and create space for greater peace, dignity, and connection. We help individuals and families navigate caregiving, hospice, and end-of-life transitions by providing guidance, advocacy, resources, and practical support along the way.</p>
  <p>Sometimes that means helping people have important conversations. Sometimes it means connecting them with services, exploring options, or bringing clarity to difficult decisions. Sometimes it means helping gather the information and plans that allow families to move forward with confidence.</p>
  <p>We chose the name <em>La Loba Pathways</em> because it reflects our belief that life's final transitions are not defined solely by loss. They are also opportunities to honor what has been lived, strengthen connections, and approach the future with greater understanding, intention, and wholeness.</p>

      <p>Mikeila Morgan is an advocate, planner, and founder of La Loba Pathways. She has always been drawn to helping people navigate complex situations, organize information, and find clarity when life feels uncertain.</p>
      <p>With a professional background in planning and project management, Mikeila combines practical problem-solving with a deeply human approach to support individuals and families facing caregiving responsibilities, aging-related challenges, serious illness, hospice, and end-of-life decisions. Whether she is helping someone understand available resources, preparing for difficult conversations, or accompanying a family member to a medical appointment, her goal is to ensure people feel informed, supported, and empowered.</p>
      <p>Mikeila's approach is shaped by a simple belief: uncertainty often creates more anxiety than the thing itself. When facing something important or unfamiliar, she finds comfort in understanding the path ahead — learning what to expect, identifying available options, and becoming familiar with the process. Over time, she realized that many people experience caregiving, serious illness, hospice, and end-of-life planning in the same way. The fear is often not the situation itself, but the uncertainty surrounding it.</p>
      <p>She doesn't believe people avoid these conversations because they lack courage. Rather, she believes they avoid them because the path feels unclear. Through advocacy, education, organization, and guidance, Mikeila helps illuminate that path so individuals and families can move forward with greater confidence and peace of mind.</p>
      <p>The story of La Loba resonates deeply with her because it is, in many ways, a story of restoring wholeness. By helping families gather information, resources, wishes, documents, plans, and conversations into one place, she strives to bring clarity and order to times that can otherwise feel overwhelming.</p>
      <p>Mikeila believes that no one should have to navigate life's most difficult transitions alone, and she is committed to helping others move forward with greater confidence, dignity, and peace.</p>
